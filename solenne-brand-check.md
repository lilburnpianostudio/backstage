# Brand Name Check: "Solenne Naturals"

**Date checked:** October 29, 2026
**Proposed use:** Handmade soap, body oils, and lotions (Georgia, USA). Tagline: "Elegance Rooted in Nature."
**Not legal advice.** This is a preliminary screen only. Have a trademark attorney run a full clearance search before investing in branding.

## Bottom line

**Do not treat the name as clear yet. Verdict: HIGH RISK, pending attorney review.**

Two findings drive this:

1. **A pending federal application for SOLÉNE (Credo Brands LLC, Serial 99762476, filed April 14, 2026) covers non-medicated soaps, cosmetic soaps, and skin care preparations in Class 3.** It is nearly identical to "Solenne" and covers the same goods. "Naturals" is a descriptive word and adds little protection against a likelihood-of-confusion refusal. Credo Brands' earlier filing date would give it priority over any application you file now.
2. **An existing business, Solenne Fragrance (Tampa, Florida; wholesale on Faire), sells hand-blended, naturally derived scented and body-care products under the Solenne name.** It may hold common-law rights even without a registration. That is a related-goods conflict in the same industry.

## 6. Recommendation

**Is the name safe?** Not yet. Red flags: (1) Credo Brands' pending Class 3 SOLÉNE application for soaps and skin care; (2) Solenne Fragrance, an established same-name business in related goods; (3) several other pending Class 3 marks (SOLÉN, SOLYNE) that crowd the field and make a clean registration harder.

**Suggested path:**

1. **Pause on branding spend** (labels, packaging, signage).
2. **Hire a trademark attorney for a full clearance opinion** (typically $300 to $1,000 for a search and opinion). Ask specifically about Credo Brands and Solenne Fragrance.
3. **Check the facts on Credo Brands:** Is it selling? Is the application likely to be approved? If it is a startup you could approach, a coexistence agreement is sometimes possible.
4. **Consider a more distinctive name** if the attorney agrees there is real risk. A coined or less common word is cheaper to protect than a lightly modified existing one. Rebranding later costs far more than choosing now.

**If the name is cleared, register in this order:**

1. **File the federal trademark application** (or at least secure the domain first the same day). Filing date sets priority, and an intent-to-use (Section 1(b)) filing lets you claim the date before launch.
2. **Domain:** `.com` and the common variants (about $10 to $20 each per year).
3. **Social handles and Etsy shop:** Free; claim all of them the same day as the domain.
4. **Georgia LLC:** $100 filing fee with the Secretary of State. File it after clearance, since the LLC name offers no trademark protection.
5. **Georgia trade name check or a DBA** if you will operate under a name different from the LLC.

**Federal trademark cost and timeline:**

- USPTO fee: $350 per class under the current fee schedule (the old TEAS Plus/Standard tiers were replaced in January 2025 by a single base application). Verify the current fee at https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/fees-payment-information before filing. Additional fees apply for free-text goods descriptions or insufficient information.
- Classes: Class 3 is the core (soap, body oil, lotion). Class 35 (online retail) is optional and can wait. Class 44 is not needed for products.
- Timeline: first examination takes roughly 6 to 8 months after filing, and registration typically takes 12 to 18 months in total if no objections arise. Add time for any office action or opposition.
- Total budget: roughly $350 for DIY single class, or $800 to $1,500 with attorney or service help.

**File yourself or use a service?** A single-class filing for a clear, distinctive mark is manageable yourself. Because this name already faces a close prior-filed mark, use an attorney: a refusal based on likelihood of confusion is hard to overcome alone, and a mistake in identifying the goods cannot be fixed after filing. Online filing services are cheaper than attorneys but typically do not give legal advice on clearance.
