## Key terminology: "waterproof" vs. "water- and oil-resistant"

White BOPP (biaxially oriented polypropylene) film is the standard answer for lotion and body oil bottles. It resists water and oils, but one vendor (Lightning Labels) explicitly says its BOPP is *not* rated for prolonged submersion. For labels on a shower-side lotion bottle, that is fine. Always order a sample and test it (rub with oil, wet it, rub again) before committing. Plain paper and kraft paper labels are not water-resistant; StickerGiant states its paper roll labels are not recommended where water resistance is needed.

### Takeaways for labels

1. **Best fit for a first run of 50-125 labels:** Avery WePrint (no minimum, waterproof film, kraft option) and Lightning Labels ("no minimum", BOPP recommended for bath and body). Both need a configurator quote.
2. **Lowest published price signal:** Jukebox Print at about $25 per 100 matte BOPP labels (promo; confirm current offer).
3. **StickerGiant** has the clearest published proofing and file-prep documentation, but a 125-label minimum means you will likely buy more labels than a 50-unit soap launch needs.
4. **Sticker Mule** is an easy, fast option with a BOPP label product, but quantity and finish details need to be checked in the configurator.
5. **Shipping to Georgia:** None of the vendors reported surcharges for Georgia. Free shipping thresholds seen: Avery free over $50; Lightning Labels free ground shipping on online orders; Sticker Mule promo pages cite free shipping. Confirm at checkout.

### Label stock to ask for by name

- **"White BOPP (biaxially oriented polypropylene) film, matte or satin, permanent acrylic adhesive."** Ask for the stock name and thickness (about 2-3.5 mil is typical for BOPP; the 3.5 mil figure was not verified).
- Alternatives: Avery WePrint "matte white vinyl film" or "glossy white vinyl film" (waterproof, oil-resistant); Sticker Mule BOPP label (about 6.4 mil).
- For kraft look on lotion bottles: ask whether they print on **kraft-look BOPP**, since regular kraft paper will absorb oil and water.
- Request a **laminate or overlay** if the label will sit in a shower: matte or gloss lamination improves rub resistance.
- Ask whether the adhesive is rated for **cosmetic and oil** exposure.

### File prep checklist for the printer

- [ ] Exact trim size confirmed (3 in x 2 in, or your final bottle-fitted size). Measure the bottle flat label area.
- [ ] 1/8 in (0.125 in) bleed on all sides.
- [ ] All text and logos at least 1/8 in inside the cut line (safe area).
- [ ] Die-cut line as a separate vector path: 0.5 pt stroke, 100% magenta, named "CutContour" or "dieline" (StickerGiant's convention; confirm per vendor).
- [ ] Fonts outlined or embedded; vector logos (AI, SVG, EPS or vector PDF).
- [ ] Raster images at 300 dpi at final size.
- [ ] Color mode CMYK; convert from RGB and check that Solenne's brand colors survive the conversion. Avoid thin hairlines and very small text (minimum about 6 pt, 1 pt strokes).
- [ ] Rich black vs. flat black decided for dark backgrounds.
- [ ] Clear labels: vector art with no transparency (StickerGiant requirement).
- [ ] Export as print-ready PDF (PDF/X-4 if offered) plus the native or vector source file.
- [ ] Required content on label verified (product name, net weight/volume, ingredients, business name and address). Legal review recommended for cosmetic labeling.
- [ ] Digital proof reviewed carefully at 100%: spelling, spacing, cut line. Order a physical sample or small test run before the full print.
- [ ] Waterproof test: stick a label on a bottle, wet it, apply oil, rub, repeat after 24 hours.
